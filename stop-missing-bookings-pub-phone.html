How to stop missing bookings when the pub phone rings.

Practical fixes for pubs, bars and restaurants: what to do today, this week and this month.

14 November 2026 · 5 minute read

Friday, half seven. The bar is three deep, the kitchen is calling service, and the phone behind the till starts ringing. Nobody can get to it. By the time it stops, whoever was calling has probably rung the next place on their list.

Most of the calls a pub or restaurant misses aren’t nuisance calls. They’re people trying to give you money: a table for Sunday lunch, a birthday for twenty, the function room for a christening. Here are practical ways to catch more of them, starting with the free ones.

1. Find out when you’re actually missing calls

Before you change anything, look at your phone’s call log or your provider’s online account for the last two weeks. Note the missed calls and the times. Many venues find the same pattern: missed calls bunch up at the busiest hour of service, and again after closing, when people are at home making plans.

That tells you where to put your effort. A gap at 7pm on a Friday needs a different answer from a gap at 10am on a Monday.

2. Make your voicemail work harder

If a call does go to voicemail, the message is your second chance. Keep it short and useful:

  • Say your name and that you’re busy with guests, not closed.
  • Give your opening and kitchen hours, because thatthat’s what half of callers wantrsquo;s what many callers want.
  • Ask them to leave their name, number, date, time and how many people, and say when you’ll call back.
  • If you take bookings online, say where.

Then actually check it, at a set time every day, and ring people back the same day. A booking request that sits in voicemail until Tuesday is a lost booking.

3. Put the answers where people look

Lots of calls are questions you’ve answered a hundred times: Are dogs allowed? Is there parking? Do you have gluten-free options? What time does the kitchen close?

Put those answers on your website, your Google listing and your Facebook page, in plain words near the top. Every question answered there is a call that doesn’t need answering at 7pm on a Friday.

4. Make one person responsible for the phone at peak times

When everyone can answer the phone, often nobody does. During your busiest hours, give one person the job and put the phone where they can reach it. Keep a booking book or tablet next to it so the details go straight in, not on the back of a receipt.

This costs nothing, but it only works when you have the staff to spare, and those are exactly the nights you don’t.

5. Offer online booking, but don’t rely on it alone

An online booking system takes care of the simple tables. But the most valuable enquiries, like big groups, private hire, set menus and anything with a question attached, still tend to come by phone. People want to talk to someone before they book a room for fifty.
